  1. I

    Judgement

    Every entry is read in full. The System weighs what was done, how much of it, and how hard. It does not weigh what was meant, planned or felt.

    Detail is rewarded because detail can be weighed. A count, a weight, a distance, a length of time: each gives the System something to hold. Vague words give it nothing, and nothing is what they earn.

    Exaggeration is noted. Each day is weighed against the days before it.

  2. II

    Body, Mind and Will

    Three measures are kept. Each rises by level, and every level asks more than the one before.

    Body
    Physical exertion. Labour, training, distance, endurance: effort the body pays for.
    Mind
    Deliberate mental effort. Study, practice, problems worked through to the end. What is merely consumed is not effort.
    Will
    Discipline through consistency. Will does not grow from any single deed. It grows with each day recorded in a row. A day left unrecorded wears it down.
  3. III

    The daily limit

    A day can hold only so much. The System weighs at most [N] entries in a day. Anything written beyond that is recorded, but not weighed.

    Gains within a single day are also bounded. Ten hours of one deed is not ten times one hour. The day ends at midnight, in the time zone the record keeps.

  4. IV

    Skills

    Skills are not chosen. When the same deed is recorded day after day, the System gives it a name.

    A named skill has ranks. Each rank asks for more days, and harder ones. A skill that is neglected weakens. Left long enough, it is Lost, and must be earned again from nothing.

  5. V

    Classes

    A class is assigned, not picked. It comes only when Will is strong enough to carry one.

    The class is drawn from the record itself: what was done most, and how it was done. It grows with the record. When the record changes, the class takes a different form.

  6. VI

    Weekly judgement

    On the seventh day, the System looks back over the week as a whole. It weighs balance and constancy, not single deeds.

    The verdict is given once. It is kept in the Chronicle and is not revised.

Not everything is written here.
